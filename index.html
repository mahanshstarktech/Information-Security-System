02

CIA Triad — The Foundation of Security

Core Concept · Every Paper

Three Pillars of Information Security

CIA has nothing to do with the spy agency — it stands for the three things that keep your data safe: kept secret (C), not tampered (I), and always available (A).
PillarFormal DefinitionLaymanExample of Violation
Confidentiality (C)Only authorized parties can access the information"Your secret stays secret"Eavesdropper reads your encrypted message
Integrity (I)Data is accurate and has not been tampered with"What you receive is exactly what was sent"Hacker modifies your bank transfer amount
Availability (A)Systems and data are accessible when needed"The service is up when you need it"DOS attack takes down a hospital's servers

Additional Security Properties

  • Authentication: Proving who you claim to be (your password, OTP, fingerprint)
  • Non-repudiation: Sender cannot deny having sent a message — digital signatures provide this
  • Authorization: Controlling what authenticated users can do
⚡
Exam Trick — "Three Security Principles" Question

2023-Aug Part A Q1 asks "Explain the three security principles." Always answer: C (Confidentiality), I (Integrity), A (Availability) + define each in 1 line + give 1 real-world example of each. This gives you perfect 2 marks. The 2023-24 paper has "Describe security goals in detail" as 10-mark — same CIA structure but expand each with algorithms that enforce them.

03

Security Services & Mechanisms

Core Theory · Part A/B

Security Services (What we want)

A security service is a processing or communication service that enhances the security of data processing and information transfer. Defined by ITU-T X.800:

ServiceWhat it providesAlgorithm/Mechanism
AuthenticationAssurance of identity of communicating entitiesPasswords, digital signatures, Kerberos
Access ControlPrevents unauthorized use of resourcesACLs, Firewalls
Data ConfidentialityProtection of transmitted data from passive attacksDES, AES, RSA encryption
Data IntegrityAssurance that received data has not been alteredHash functions, MACs
Non-RepudiationPrevents sender/receiver denying a transmitted messageDigital signatures
AvailabilitySystem resources accessible when neededIDS, redundant systems

Security Mechanisms (How we achieve services)

Mechanisms are techniques that implement security services. Key mechanisms include:

  • Encipherment: Transforming data using encryption algorithms (DES, AES, RSA)
  • Digital Signature: Data appended to message that proves authenticity
  • Access Control: Rights, permissions, and capabilities
  • Data Integrity: Hash functions to detect modification
  • Authentication Exchange: Protocols to verify identity (Kerberos)
  • Traffic Padding: Inserting dummy bits to defeat traffic analysis
  • Routing Control: Selecting physically secure routes
  • Notarization: Trusted third party for non-repudiation
04

Security Attacks — Active vs Passive

🔥 Every Paper — Must Know

Two Categories of Attacks

A passive attacker is like someone secretly reading your diary — they don't change anything, just spy. An active attacker is like someone breaking into your house and rearranging furniture — they actively interfere.
AspectPassive AttackActive Attack
NatureEavesdropping; monitoring transmissionsModifying, replaying, or creating messages
GoalObtain information being transmittedDisrupt or corrupt communication
DetectionVery hard — leaves no traceEasier — disrupts communication flow
PreventionEncryption (confidentiality)Authentication, integrity checks
CIA targetConfidentialityIntegrity, Availability, Authentication

Types of Passive Attacks

  • Release of message contents: Reading unencrypted messages — like reading someone's open postcard
  • Traffic Analysis: Even if encrypted, attacker can observe who talks to whom, how often, message length — patterns reveal information

Types of Active Attacks

  • Masquerade: Pretending to be someone else — like using a fake ID
  • Replay: Capturing a valid transmission and re-sending it later — like replaying a login request
  • Modification: Altering message content — changing "Transfer ₹100" to "Transfer ₹10000"
  • Denial of Service (DOS): Preventing legitimate access — flooding a server with requests
Key Distinction (2-mark answer format) Passive Attack: Intercepts but does NOT modify data. Goal = confidentiality breach.
Prevention: Encryption → makes intercepted data unreadable

Active Attack: Intercepts AND modifies/injects/deletes data. Goal = integrity/availability breach.
Prevention: Authentication + integrity checks (MACs, digital signatures)

Solved PYQs — Introduction Topics

Part A · All Years
📋 2024 + 2023 PAPERS · PART A Q1Topic: Active vs Passive Attacks2 Marks
Differentiate between active and passive attacks.
Passive Attack: Attacker monitors/eavesdrops on transmissions without modifying data. Goal is confidentiality breach. Hard to detect. Examples: traffic analysis, message interception. Prevention: encryption.

Active Attack: Attacker modifies, replays, or creates false data. Goal is integrity/availability breach. Easier to detect. Examples: masquerade, replay, DOS. Prevention: authentication + MACs.
📋 2023-AUG · PART A Q1Topic: Three Security Principles2 Marks
Explain the three security principles.
The three security principles are the CIA Triad:

1. Confidentiality: Only authorized parties can access information. Ensured by: encryption (DES, AES, RSA).
2. Integrity: Data is accurate and unaltered in transit. Ensured by: hash functions, MACs, digital signatures.
3. Availability: Systems and data accessible when needed. Ensured by: redundancy, IDS, firewall.
📋 ALL PAPERS · PART ATopic: Stream vs Block Cipher2 Marks
Differentiate between stream cipher and block cipher.
Stream Cipher: Encrypts plaintext one bit/byte at a time. Uses a keystream generator. Faster, simpler. Example: RC4, A5/1 (used in GSM). Suitable for real-time communication.

Block Cipher: Encrypts plaintext in fixed-size blocks (64 or 128 bits). Processes entire block together. More widely used. Example: DES (64-bit blocks), AES (128-bit blocks).

Key difference: Stream → operates bit-by-bit; Block → operates on fixed blocks.
📋 2025 PAPER · PART A Q1Topic: Cryptography vs Cryptanalysis2 Marks
What is the difference between Cryptography and Cryptanalysis?
Cryptography: The science of designing methods to protect information — creating ciphers, keys, and protocols that make data unreadable to unauthorized parties. (Making locks)

Cryptanalysis: The science of breaking cryptographic systems — analyzing ciphers to recover plaintext without the key. (Breaking locks)

Together, they form Cryptology. Cryptography makes secure systems; Cryptanalysis tests and breaks them to find weaknesses.
📋 2023 PAPERS · PART ATopic: Avalanche Effect2 Marks
What is avalanche effect?
The avalanche effect is a desirable property of cryptographic algorithms: a small change in input (plaintext or key) causes a large, unpredictable change in the output (ciphertext).

Like an avalanche — you disturb one small snowflake and the entire mountain shifts. Change 1 bit of your password, and your encrypted message looks completely different.
Quantified: Ideally, changing 1 bit in input should change ~50% of output bits. DES and AES both exhibit strong avalanche effect. This property prevents attackers from predicting ciphertext changes, making the cipher resistant to cryptanalysis.
